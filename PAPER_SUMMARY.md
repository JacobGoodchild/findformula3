# Paper summary (plain English)

*Written on 3 November 2026. Nothing has been uploaded, posted or e-mailed anywhere. The paper is in `paper/main.pdf`; suggested Zenodo wording is in `paper/ZENODO.md`.*

## What I did this time
Last night's session left 13 results. Today I did not look for anything new. I (1) opened every paper we cited and checked the titles, authors and the exact wording of each conjecture; (2) searched the literature again for every result, properly this time; (3) had each proof read by a fresh "referee" that had never seen our work, and had each number recomputed by fresh code; and (4) wrote up only what survived, as one paper.

## What is in the paper
Title: *Answers to some recent conjectures and questions in classical analysis, with computer assistance.* About 20 pages. Statements are clearly labelled as **theorem**, **computer-assisted theorem** (a written argument plus a finite computation that is described exactly), or **numerical result** (never presented as a theorem).

1. **A proof of two conjectures of Fan (2026) about "Riesz capacity".** (Roughly: how best to put three points on a circle to make a certain interaction energy as large as possible, and what happens for the corners of regular polygons with an odd number of sides.) Fan could prove only half of it. The proof is a calculus argument plus one small inequality. It was refereed twice by fresh referees (both: correct, only small wording fixes). It relies on three results that Fan's paper quotes from other authors (Björck; Clark–Laugesen), which I used as stated there but did not re-prove. **This is the strongest result.**
2. **A published conjecture is false.** Xu and Zhao (2026) conjectured that a threshold number Gr(ℓ) grows like 3^ℓ (between 0.47·3^ℓ and 0.477·3^ℓ). Their own value for ℓ=5 already breaks the lower bound (113 is below 114.2). I computed Gr(ℓ) exactly for ℓ up to 10 (rigorously, with interval arithmetic; a second, independent program agrees) and numerically up to 14; the numbers suggest growth like e^ℓ, not 3^ℓ.
3. **A computer-assisted proof for a "positivity" question about the exponential.** Fasi and Higham (2018) conjectured that a certain reciprocal polynomial always has positive coefficients; Scardino (2026) showed this is false in general but left open the case that matters in practice (the "diagonal" one). I proved it, one by one, for every odd size from 3 to 361 using certified computer arithmetic, and found by computation where it first fails for a given size (about twice the size plus 12–14). It is not a proof for all sizes, and the method cannot reach all sizes.
4. **Explicit values for sums of products of harmonic numbers** (Chen–Nguyen asked for them): exact computer reduction to multiple zeta values for orders up to 9, and explicit closed forms for orders up to 8. The closed forms were found numerically (by integer-relation search) and checked to 40+ digits; I could not turn that last step into a proof, so they are labelled "numerical identification". The method itself is standard theory; what is new is the explicit values.
5. **Smaller items:** a derivation of Finch's conjectured constants for Abel's equation (it turns out to follow quickly from classical facts, and the paper says so); an exact series and the value of a second derivative that Finch asked for; three half-iterate values of exponentials that Finch asked for; answers to two of the three open problems of Wiśniewska on Gurland's gamma-function ratio (elementary: a Taylor series has a larger radius of convergence than she knew); and numerical values of two "completely monotonic degrees" that Krasniqi said were open.
6. **Open problems** I could not settle (Section 9), including the all-sizes version of item 3 and a sharp inequality for Gurland's ratio.

## What I dropped, and why
* **Four of the 13 results turned out to be already known**, so they are not claimed:
  * the Habib–Hildebrand conjecture on an ODE: the same authors proved it four days after posting it (I only found out when I searched their follow-up paper);
  * the Chavan–Kobayashi–Layja conjecture on "t-values": proved by Charlton (the paper says so explicitly);
  * Finch's two identities between Abel functions: Finch himself explains them in a corrigendum at the end of that paper;
  * the Samart Mahler-measure conjecture: the author Zheng's own paper contains the same finding (and I had misnamed its author).
  (My proofs of the first two were checked by the referees and are correct; they are simply not new.)
* **One number that did not answer its question** (a probability for random polynomials, Gasull's problem 21): it was only a numerical value, not the asymptotics that the problem asks for. Dropped.

## What changed after checking (corrections and downgrades)
* "Proved" was downgraded to "numerical identification" for the harmonic-sum closed forms, and the Gregory values were upgraded to rigorous only after an independent program with interval arithmetic reproduced them.
* Mistakes found and fixed: a wrong author for the Samart paper; a quoted formula (Finch's second addendum) that I had read wrongly from a garbled PDF; a statement "Hoffman proved N=1,2,3" that I had written from memory and that is not what the paper says; a wrong sanity-check figure for the Gurland series (1e-10 where the truth is about 1e-55); a wrong growth estimate for the Padé thresholds; an over-strong "strictly increasing" claim in the Riesz proof (now "nondecreasing", which is all that is needed); and my first description of what Scardino proved (he proves only "eventual" positivity for large sizes).
* The Padé referees showed that three things must be certified exactly (no repeated zero, exactly one positive real zero, a strict gap in size); the overnight logs did not record them, so I wrote a new check and ran it for every size up to 361 (no failures).
* A paper's own table has a misprint (a value 5065 that should be 5605.2); I noted it.

## An honest assessment of how significant the paper is
This is a modest paper. Its one clear new theorem is the proof of Fan's two conjectures; it solves a question that the author explicitly could not finish, which could be the core of a short note. The refutation of the Gregory conjecture is real but almost immediate once one looks at the authors' own table; the new exact values are genuinely new but of narrow interest. The Padé result is a real partial answer to an open question, but only for finitely many cases, and its method has a built-in limit. Several other items (Finch's constants, Gurland, Krasniqi's degrees, the harmonic sums) are correct, checked, and useful to the people who asked, but are elementary or numerical, and a referee would probably see them as footnotes. The weak spots are the amount of "computer-assisted" and "numerical" content, the dependence of the Riesz proof on three quoted results that I did not re-prove, and the fact that all of it was checked only by AI referees and software, not by a human specialist. I would publish it as a preprint with the AI disclosure as written, and treat the Riesz proof as the part most worth sending to an expert (Fan, or someone in potential theory) for a human check.
